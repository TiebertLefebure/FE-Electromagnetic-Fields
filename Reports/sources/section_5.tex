\section*{Question and choice of topic}
\assignmentquestion{\QuestionFive}
\answer{\AnsFive}
The additional investigation was a free choice. We chose the
\textbf{arrangement of the three single cores combined with the screen bonding}: \emph{what is the
best arrangement for a given specification?} It answers a practical design question and, unlike
most alternatives, can be \emph{validated}: the NKT datasheet gives the 90\,\(^\circ\)C rating for
six arrangement/bonding combinations under the same laying conditions.

\section*{Cases and method}

each with both-end bonding (\(U=0\) per screen) and single-point bonding (\(I=0\)). All cables are
1 m below the surface (arrangement centre). Phase B is the middle cable of the flat formations.

\textbf{FE ampacity} (resolution \texttt{Ampacity} of \afile). For each case GetDP (1) solves the
coupled problem at 350 A, which gives all values of the table and the maps; (2) searches the current
for which the hot spot is 90\,\(^\circ\)C with the secant method on \(I^2\), starting from
\(I_1=350\sqrt{75/(T_0-15)}\) (temperature rise proportional to \(I^2\)). Each step is a full
coupled \(\sigma(T)\) solve; at most \AmpStepsMax\ steps bring the hot spot within 0.01 K of 90\,\(^\circ\)C.
Changing the current only scales the loss density by \((I/350)^2\): for a given \(\sigma\) the magnetic
problem is linear in \(I\).

\section*{Discussion}
\textbf{Validation.} The FE reproduces the datasheet trends: close trefoil and close flat are
equivalent (\Amptrefoil/\Ampflat\ A both ends, \Amptrefoilsp/\Ampflatsp\ A single point; NKT 350 A
for all four), and spacing the flat formation by 70 mm raises the rating, much more with
single-point bonding. All six FE ampacities lie within \dNktMin\,\% and \dNktMax\,\% of NKT's. The FE
tends to be slightly low, consistently with Section 4 (convective ground surface instead of IEC's
isothermal one; FE screen losses include eddy currents).

\textbf{Physics.} Spacing the cables reduces their mutual heating (each cable sits less in its
neighbours' temperature field). With both-end bonding, however, the screen-loop area grows with the
spacing: the induced screen voltage \(\propto\ln(2s/d)\) and the circulating losses rise from
\Pstrefoil\ to \Psflatsev\ kW/km (\(P_s\) column), eating most of the thermal gain
(\Ampflatsev\ A instead of \Ampflatsevsp\ A). Single-point bonding removes the circulating current, so
the full thermal benefit of spacing is obtained; the price is a standing voltage that also grows
with spacing, and a 3 times wider trench. The inductance grows with spacing (\(\ln s\)), which
increases the series voltage drop.

\textbf{Best arrangement.} For the specified 350 A, the datasheet trefoil with both ends bonded is
at its limit (\Tcnl\,\(^\circ\)C with \(h=10\), within 3 K of 90\,\(^\circ\)C); it is the most compact,
with the lowest inductance and external magnetic field. Single-point bonding of the same trefoil
brings it to \Tctrefoilsp\,\(^\circ\)C at no space cost. If more current is needed in the same trench conditions, a spaced flat formation with
single-point or cross-bonded screens gives the largest ampacity (\Ampflatsevsp\ A).
