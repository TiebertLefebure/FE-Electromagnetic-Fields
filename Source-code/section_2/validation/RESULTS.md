## Q2.4: field assessment

The healthy maximum is **3.289 kV/mm RMS**. The analytical outer-interface value is **1.774 kV/mm RMS**. Both are below the adopted external screening benchmarks of 8 and 4 kV/mm RMS, respectively (Eskom 240-56030625, Rev. 3, §3.3(i), Annex B). **That specification covers 44–132 kV cables, not this 33 kV NKT product.** These are explicitly adopted comparison limits, not NKT guarantees or universal XLPE breakdown strengths. The selected datasheet gives no admissible local-field values; manufacturer-specific certification remains unestablished.

## Q2.5: axial gas-channel defect

Phase A contains a 0.5 mm diameter circular void at radius 10.3 mm on the +x radial cut. The fine result gives **6.1152 kV/mm peak in the void** and **5.9759 kV/mm peak in XLPE**. The void maximum changes by 0.21% on the last refinement. Open `radial_comparison.pos` and `results/void/view.geo`.

The 2D circular void represents an infinitely long axial channel, not a spherical bubble. Its size/location are controlled investigation parameters, not manufacturer defects or tolerances. Gas permittivity is 1 and conductivity is zero before discharge. **Withstand is not established:** this linear model contains no ionisation, space charge, discharge inception, pressure dependence or ageing. XLPE screening limits must not be applied to the gas. A discharge model or appropriate test data are needed to decide whether this defective cable withstands service.

## Q2.6: current densities and conservation

Gmsh views contain |σE| and |jωεE| in A/m². The radial CSV includes both and complex E components. These are transverse charging/leakage currents; the 415 A longitudinal load belongs to Section 3.

Maximum free-node weak residual / phase-A terminal current: **1.282e-10**. Phase-A terminal imbalance: **1.254e-10**. Largest independent circular-contour flux discrepancy: **0.100%**. Terminal reactions are discretely conservative; raw P1 gradients are not a pointwise flux-conforming field. The independent contour check exposes that discretisation error.

At 50 Hz, σ/(ωε) is approximately 3.20×10⁵ in the semiconducting regions and 1.44×10⁻¹⁰ in XLPE. Conduction dominates in the screens, displacement in XLPE/gas; total normal current is continuous in the weak solution. Phase-A peak charging current is approximately **1.8968 A/km** (divide by √2 for RMS). Tiny conductive losses are not a prediction of all real dielectric losses: the adopted XLPE model has no dielectric-loss tangent.

## Q2.7: capacitance and frequency

Computed C_A = **0.224075 µF/km**, 6.64% below NKT’s **maximum** 0.24 µF/km. The maximum is not an exact target for fitting. C is obtained both from terminal susceptance Im(I/V)/ω and from ∫ε|E|²dA/|V|². The implementation checks that they agree; it does not divide the three-phase energy by one phase voltage without the factor of three.

| Frequency (Hz) | C_A (µF/km) |
|---:|---:|
| 5 | 0.224074656 |
| 50 | 0.224074656 |
| 500 | 0.224074656 |
| 5000 | 0.224074644 |

With constant ε and perfectly equipotential screens the analytical C is frequency independent. Finite screen conductivity gives a weak frequency dependence; real dispersion, dielectric loss and temperature dependence are not modelled.

## Q2.8: an insulation-thickness improvement

Increasing XLPE from 8 to 9 mm while retaining the conductor and screen thicknesses gives **4.2610 kV/mm peak**, a **8.39% reduction**, and **0.205472 µF/km**. All outside interfaces shift outwards by 1 mm and the touching-trefoil spacing increases by 2 mm. This is a hypothetical design variant, not a change to the selected NKT baseline or its 415 A rating. Larger dimensions, changed screen geometry and poorer thermal transfer require separate manufacturing/thermal assessment.
